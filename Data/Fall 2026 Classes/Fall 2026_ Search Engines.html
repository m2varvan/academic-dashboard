Course Description

Calendar Description for MSE 541

This course provides an opportunity for students to learn the engineering behind search engines and how to optimize search engines to provide higher quality user experiences. This course focuses on text retrieval and web search. Topics include design and construction of retrieval systems, retrieval models, and evaluation of search engines.

View requirements for MSE 541

Search engines are prevalent in society.  We use search engines such as ChatGPT, Claude, Google, and Bing for many reasons ranging from finding simple facts to researching literature about leading treatments for diseases.   Many web sites have their own search engines, e.g. X (Twitter), Amazon, and the University of Waterloo.  Companies also often use search engines internally to help employees find information across the company.  Certain products also contain search engines for a variety purposes including the search of personal email archives, help pages, and product documentation.

I have designed this course to provide students the opportunity to learn the engineering behind search engines and how to optimize search engines to provide higher quality user experiences.  This course covers the fundamentals of search engines, which could be considered classical methods.  The methodology for optimization and evaluation, as covered in the course, is applicable to both classical and the most modern of retrieval methods.  As time permits, I we will cover modern neural approaches to retrieval, but this course will focus on classical text retrieval and its evaluation.

Learning Activities

This course places significant weight on the homework assignments.  The majority of the homework assignments require software design and implementation based on high level requirements and algorithms.  A portion of the homework will also include problems similar to those that will be found on the final exam.  The programming assignments are cumulative and require working software from the previous assignment to make the subsequent assignment also work. 

Tutorials will be used to tutor and help students successfully complete the assignments. Some tutorials early in the course will be used as makeup lectures for periods when the instructor will be away later in the term.

Foundation for Successful Participation 

Course prerequisites are MSE 240 (Algorithms and Data Structures) and MSE 251 or MSCI 252 (Probability and Statistics for Engineers) but as a 500 level course, students need to be comfortable with less structure and higher expectations for self-regulated learning.  Knowledge of linear algebra is helpful, but not required as the material from linear algebra that we use is quick to learn. Past experience has shown that students who are weak in their information systems skills, in particular computer programming, have significant difficulty with course expectations.  In other words, your average in your introduction to programming and algorithms and data structures courses should be 80 or higher, and you should enjoy the process of computer programming. 

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course students should be able to:
Identify, explain, and implement the key components of a search engine. [KB]
Implement and compute offline effectiveness measures using a test collection. [PA]
Make and justify decisions based on the outcome of experiments using statistics. [PA]
Diagnose search quality problems and suggest areas of engine improvement for future experiments. [Inv]
Design and implement a search engine and evaluation framework. [Des]
Design and conduct an experiment to test a proposed system change.[Des]

Academic honesty / working with others / use of other sources

Executive summary: I encourage you to seek out additional sources, seek help from the instructor and TAs, and form study groups to aid your learning of the course material, but it is of the highest importance that you always acknowledge the sources of the ideas in your work.  If the idea is not your own, you must acknowledge.  You must always acknowledge assistance of others in your work.  If you need to copy something as part of an answer, you must quote it and cite the source.

Details:

For the purposes of academic honesty, ChatGPT, Google Bard/Gemini, Bing “Chat”/Co-pilot, and other artificial intelligence (AI) tools are to be considered the equivalent of a human.  In other words, your behavior with AI must be considered acceptable if you were to pose the question or prompt or query, etc. to another human.  For example, getting an AI to help you understand a topic is okay, but getting an AI to give you an answer is wrong.  Likewise, if you do consult an AI for help, you must declare this in your work as you would another human. 

All work must be your own. You must acknowledge assistance from the web, books, AI, people, and others.  No material may be copied from any source except when you must copy something as part of your answer, and in these cases, you must quote the copied material and cite the source.  Turning in the work of others (including AI) as your own; failure to acknowledge sources and assistance and discussions; and providing answers to others are violations of academic integrity.  All violations of academic integrity will be reported to the Associate Dean.

You may discuss homework with others/AI, but you may not exchange work or answers.  For work involving computer code (e.g. Java, C#, etc.), this means that your work may not contain the code, or portions of code, of another person, AI, or book or website etc. unless otherwise specified by the homework assignment.  As much as possible, discussions of computer programs should be at the level of pseudo-code and not at the level of real computer code.

You are expected to be able to demonstrate that you produced the work in your homework.  The instructor may ask you to show your work, notes, drafts, and so forth for your submitted work.  Likewise, if there is a question about the originality of your work, you may be asked to submit to an oral exam regarding the material.  If you are unable to demonstrate that your submitted work is your own, the instructor will consider submitting an allegation of possible academic misconduct to the Associate Dean.

How do you work with others etc. and still be academically honest (ethical)?

  1. If you have a question about what a problem means, ask the instructor.  Do not ask AI or others to explain a problem to you. The instructor will always respond to requests for clarification of homework problems.  You cannot be found guilty of academic misconduct if the help comes from the instructor.  Please post your question on Campuswire.  Many other students are likely to have the same question.  Note: Please do not contact the TA directly.  Please post your question to Campuswire, and there the instructor can respond or verify that the TA or classmate’s answer is correct.

  2. Attempt to do all work by yourself first.  If you solve a problem using only your brain and hard work, then you have no one else to acknowledge.

  3. If you need help, please use Campuswire to ask for help.  Post your question to be visible by everyone and not only the instructor/TA. If you are concerned your question contains an answer, please post only for the eyes of the instructor/TA.

  4. Work with others, but never copy.  Working in groups can be a great way to learn material better and faster.  You can also turn to original sources and other books, but you must do ALL of the following (a, b, c, and d):

    1. You must put away all material containing a solution (group notes, internet webpages, solution guides, etc.) and write-up your own solution.  If you cannot write up a solution from start to finish without looking at group notes, then the work is not your own, but someone else’s.  Go back, study the problem some more, then try to write up your solution.  Repeat until you can do it without using the notes.  All students must be able to pass an oral examination that demonstrates that they have submitted their own work.

    2. You must acknowledge your sources whether a written source or a person or an AI.  It is often obvious to your instructor and TAs when you have copied an answer, no matter how obscure you might think your source is.  You must acknowledge anyone or AI you discussed a problem or assignment with, and this includes the instructor and TAs.

    3. You must also acknowledge the nature of your source.  If you uncovered a solution to a problem, you must acknowledge that!

    4. You do not need to acknowledge the course lectures or the course textbook unless you are directly quoting them.

    5. Write your acknowledgments either at the beginning or end of your problem write-up. Examples:

      1. I discussed this problem with Bob and Beth.

      2. The instructor helped me with this problem during office hours.

      3. I had ChatGPT explain to me about vector space retrieval.

      4. Working with Sue, we solved this problem together.

      5. I found an answer to this problem in XXXX, by X Y, page X.

      6. Accidently in trying to understand this problem using ChatGPT, ChatGPT gave me the answer.

  5. If in doubt about what is allowed for collaboration, you need to ask the instructor for clarification.
